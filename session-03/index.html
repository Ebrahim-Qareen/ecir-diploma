    The Detection & Analysis phase

    The second NIST phase is the bridge between being ready and acting. It starts when something is noticed and ends when a confirmed, prioritised incident is handed over.

    What INE says

    The Detection and Analysis phase of Incident Response (IR) is a critical stage where security teams identify, validate, and assess potential security incidents. It bridges the gap between preparation and active response, ensuring that threats are detected early, analyzed thoroughly, and escalated appropriately for containment and remediation.

    Detection & Analysis (D&A)term
    Definition
    The IR phase that finds possible incidents, checks if they are real, and measures how big and how urgent they are.
    Why it exists
    Tools and people notice many strange things. Only some are attacks. Someone must decide which ones — fast, and with evidence.
    Where used
    Every SOC shift: alert queues, triage, investigations, escalation to Tier 2 and the IR team.
    Example
    A login alert arrives; the analyst checks the user, the source IP and what happened next, then decides "incident — Critical".
    SOC example
    At 02:31:47 Wazuh raises a level-15 alert on LNX-APP01. The Tier 1 analyst confirms the login worked and escalates to Tier 2 — that is D&A in five minutes.
    Detect = notice

    A rule fires, a user calls, a partner warns you. Detection only says "something might be wrong". A rule firing is not proof.

    Analyse = decide

    Is it real? How did they get in? How far did it go? Analysis turns a signal into a decision — with evidence for every answer.

    Game 1Detect or analyse?Eight moments from the night. Drop each card into its box: noticing something, or deciding what it means.
    Detect — notice it
    Analyse — decide what it means
    Signals vs decisions. Alerts, calls and firewall lines are signals — detection. Checking intel, confirming, scoping and rating severity are decisions — analysis and triage.
    Keep this

    Detect → analyse and triage → scope and classify → enable response. Only confirmed, prioritised incidents leave this phase.

    Events, alerts and incidents

    Three words, three filters. Mixing them up wastes analyst time — or hides a breach. Session 2 gave you NIST's words; today you add the detection view: the alert in the middle.

    What INE says
    WordINE definitionKey traits
    EventAny observable occurrence in a system, network, or device.millions per day · mostly benign · logged for auditing, troubleshooting and monitoring
    AlertA notification generated by a security monitoring system (e.g. SIEM, IDS, antivirus) when a predefined rule or condition is met, often indicating potentially suspicious or malicious activity.based on rules, heuristics or baselines · may be a false positive · needs triage
    IncidentA confirmed or suspected breach of security policy or acceptable use, as determined by the analysis of alerts and associated data.actionable · needs an IR response · one or many alerts · documented and remediated
    Confirmed — or suspected?

    INE's definition says an incident is a confirmed or suspected breach. The funnel above shows where the work ends: incidents confirmed after alerts are investigated and validated. Both are true: you may declare an incident on suspicion, then analysis confirms it — or closes it.

    ▶ Event, alert or incident?

    Six things from NovaCart's night. Decide first, then click to check.

    Event

    A normal login by the deploy account, from its usual source. Raw telemetry — thousands like it every day.

    Alert

    A rule matched (rule 5551, level 10). It says "something might be wrong" — triage decides.

    Incident — confirmed

    A login from the IP that just guessed passwords, followed by sudo. Policy is breached: an attacker is using a NovaCart account.

    Event

    Normal traffic, logged. It becomes interesting only with context — source, port, volume, time.

    Incident — suspected

    Not proven yet — but a logon from the attack IP and then silence is enough to declare it. Analysis will confirm or close it.

    Alert → false positive

    The rule fired correctly, but the change was planned. Triage closes it with a note — no incident.

    Normal activity → event.

    Rule matched → alert.

    Breach confirmed → incident.

    Normal activity → event.

    Breach suspected → incident (INE: "confirmed or suspected").

    Alert, then triage → false positive.

    Why it matters in IR
    🎯 focuses the analyst on what truly matters🔕 prevents alert fatigue — signal from noise🗂 proper classification, escalation, documentation⚙️ efficient use of IR resources
    Keep this

    Events are constantly generated and collected → alerts are filtered and triggered from suspicious events → incidents are confirmed after alerts are investigated and validated.

    Logging refers to the automatic recording of system and application events that occur on a device, server, or network. These logs are generated by operating systems, applications, and network devices to capture details about activities, errors, requests, and behaviors.

    Log sourceterm

    What INE says

    Linux logging refers to the process by which Linux-based systems record events, messages, and system activity. Linux relies on a combination of text-based log files, logging daemons, and kernel-level messages to capture and store logs.

    Read one line like an analyst

    A NovaCart rule that says only "decoded as sshd" would never fire — Wazuh's rule 5700 takes every sshd line first. So NovaCart chains on Wazuh's rules: if_matched_sid 5760 and if_sid 5715.       NovaCart's Telemetry pack is complete. Eight lines to take into Session 4 — and your score for today.

      1. Detection & Analysis turns events into confirmed, prioritised incidents: detect → analyse and triage → scope and classify → hand off. Events → alerts → incidents.
      2. No log, no alert, no investigation. Endpoint = what ran and who logged on. Network = who talked to whom. Application = what they did inside the software.

      5. Generate → collect → ship → aggregate → parse → store → analyse → alert. A break at any stage is a blind spot for everything after it.
      6. Ship logs off the host — fast, encrypted, buffered — with every clock on NTP. A log that never leaves the host is a log the attacker can delete.
